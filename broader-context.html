Automation vs. Collaboration

My use of ChatGPT leans heavily into collaboration. As mentioned in earlier sections, my main uses of ChatGPT are for writing and outline assistance, coding, and applying to jobs. All of these uses require collaboration with ChatGPT because they are nuanced, specific topics that require tweaks and reworks to make sure ChatGPT knows what I want it to produce as well as making sure what it produces is up to snuff. This supports points made by Autor and Manyika (2025), where they discussed a new AI tool used by radiologists meant to improve the accuracy of diagnoses actually led to a decrease in accuracy. The radiologists often took the AI’s diagnosis over their own expert diagnoses because the AI projected confidence and its hidden inner workings didn’t let the radiologists see how it came to its conclusions. The AI also didn’t have access to patients’ medical history, which could alter conclusions. Automation did not work in this instance.

I avoid using ChatGPT like this. I could use ChatGPT to automate the tasks I listed earlier, but if I want the best possible work I still must collaborate and work with it, provide plenty of background information, and ensure correctness. There’s also been instances while coding where I’ve asked ChatGPT to generate code for a task but the code it gives doesn’t do exactly what I wanted and I’m required to make changes myself or re-prompt ChatGPT. This demonstrates that the most effective use comes from human-AI collaboration. ChatGPT provides a large base of knowledge, and I provide context and my expertise on the matters.

Technochauvinism

Technochauvinism is the idea that technology and its solutions are always better than human ideas or solutions. In the context of ChatGPT, this can appear as thinking that ChatGPT’s outputs are automatically better or more accurate than a human output. My experiences using ChatGPT do not fully support this idea though. It is useful for a wide variety of things and can project confidence, but its outputs are not inherently better and often require additional work and context that ChatGPT just doesn’t have on its own. For example, ChatGPT is known for producing confident answers that are false or just made-up, something called hallucinations.

Tools vs. System

ChatGPT can be viewed in the contexts of being a tool and being part of a larger system of technology. I have a tools-based approach to ChatGPT. I use it for specific tasks such as writing or coding. This aligns with the idea of technology as “hardware,” where the user uses the tool to complete tasks. Technology is not just a tool, though. It is a part of systems that include human context, rules, and structures that allow the technology to function. For example, ChatGPT is part of a larger system involving educational and legal policies, social norms, and then my own use of it such as prompting and refining. Without this context, the tool would not be as effective. Taking this systems approach highlights how ChatGPT’s impact goes beyond just completing tasks. It is shaping how I, and many others, learn, work, and interact with other things and technologies, rather than simply acting as just a tool to complete tasks.
